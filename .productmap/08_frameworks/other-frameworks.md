# Other Frameworks

> Draft — Frameworks / Reference

A reference guide to the most widely used product management frameworks beyond the core RICE, OKR, and Jobs-to-be-Done set. Each entry describes the framework, when to apply it, and what artefact it produces.

## HEART (Google)

**Description:** A user-centred metrics framework with five dimensions: Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention, Task Success.
**When to use:** When defining success metrics for a product or feature launch, or auditing the quality of an existing measurement system.
**Key artefact:** HEART metrics dashboard, KPI definition doc.

## AARRR / Pirate Metrics (McClure)

**Description:** A five-stage growth funnel: Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Revenue, Referral.
**When to use:** When mapping the full customer journey and identifying the biggest growth bottleneck to focus on.
**Key artefact:** AARRR funnel analysis, growth model.

## MoSCoW

**Description:** A prioritisation framework that classifies requirements as Must have, Should have, Could have, Won't have.
**When to use:** During scope definition for a release or sprint when negotiating trade-offs with stakeholders.
**Key artefact:** MoSCoW-labelled backlog or release scope document.

## Kano Model

**Description:** A model that classifies features into Basic needs, Performance needs, and Delighters based on their relationship to customer satisfaction.
**When to use:** When deciding which features to invest in and which to cut, especially when differentiating between table-stakes and differentiating capabilities.
**Key artefact:** Kano categorisation matrix, feature prioritisation output.

## RACI

**Description:** A responsibility assignment matrix: Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed.
**When to use:** When defining ownership for a project, process, or decision with multiple stakeholders.
**Key artefact:** RACI matrix.

## RAPID

**Description:** A decision-making framework: Recommend, Agree, Perform, Input, Decide.
**When to use:** When clarifying who has input vs. who makes the final call on important product or organisational decisions.
**Key artefact:** RAPID decision chart.

## DACI

**Description:** A decision framework: Driver, Approver, Contributor, Informed.
**When to use:** When structuring cross-functional decision-making, especially for decisions that span multiple teams.
**Key artefact:** DACI chart, decision brief.

## Impact Mapping

**Description:** A visual strategy technique linking business goals → actors → impacts → deliverables.
**When to use:** When aligning a roadmap to business outcomes and ensuring features are tied to user behaviour change.
**Key artefact:** Impact map diagram.

## Story Mapping

**Description:** A user journey-based backlog organisation technique by Jeff Patton that maps user activities across the top and user tasks/stories below.
**When to use:** When planning a release and needing to sequence work around user journeys rather than isolated features.
**Key artefact:** Story map, release-sliced backlog.
